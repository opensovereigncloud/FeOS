# ADR 0004: Pods are what feosd runs

**Status:** Accepted · **Date:** 2026-10-14

## Context

ADR 0001 made the pod the unit of both products. `feosd` speaks VMs today:
a client creates a VM with `feos.vm.v1` and then talks to the guest's API
through `VmInfo.vsock_path`, which gives it two APIs and two boundaries
across which versions must fit. Raw VMs have no user of their own
(ADR 0005): a VM exists to isolate containers.

The products use a pod differently, and on the node what tells them apart
is what a process's exit means:

- A **sandbox** holds one long-lived container that an agent works in. The
  container runs nothing of its own; the work comes through exec and the
  tools, often for days, and no exit ends it. Processes the agent starts in
  the background outlive the exec that started them. The sandbox system
  starts the container with `sleep infinity`.
- A **service** runs what its image says, like a Kubernetes pod: the
  image's entrypoint and command, started again when it exits, checked for
  health, with traffic only to pods that are ready. Exec is for debugging.
- A **job**, which CaaS will run as well, is done when its process exits,
  successfully or not.

A pod may hold several containers, each of which can exit on its own: a
service with a sidecar, a sandbox with a database beside it, a job with a
proxy beside it, and containers that prepare the pod before the others
start, as Kubernetes's init containers do.

Every container has a PID namespace of its own, and its process is PID 1
in it, as with Docker. A process orphaned in the container is reparented
to that PID 1: under `sleep infinity`, a background process that exits
stays a zombie. Docker has the same problem, and `docker run --init` as its
remedy.

## Decision

### The pod

A pod is a microVM that runs `feos-guest`, and one or more containers in
it. An isolated container is a pod with one container.

A pod is not a sandbox or a service: those are objects of the control
plane's API, and it runs each as pods, one for a sandbox, one or more for
a service. Their IDs go in the pod's labels, which `feosd` keeps and does
not read. A pod has no kind, and `feosd` never asks what a pod is for.

What one product needs is a capability of every pod: exec and the tools,
watches, port forwards, credential offloading, suspend and resume. What
makes a pod a sandbox or a service is the control plane's: which
capabilities a tenant may call, the agent's MCP tools mapped onto the pod's
tool and exec calls, the policies that pause, hibernate and scale to zero.
`feosd` reports what those policies need, such as the last call into a pod
and its memory, and decides none of it.

### The pod API

`feos.pod.v1.PodService` replaces `feos.vm.v1` for clients:

- `CreatePod`, with resources instead of vCPUs, kernels and balloons,
  `StartPod`, `StopPod`, `DeletePod`, `GetPod`, `ListPods`,
  `StreamPodEvents`, `SuspendPod` and `ResumePod`.
- The caller chooses the pod's ID, so a create that is delivered twice
  finds the pod it made.
- The pod's spec names its identity and its credentials (Kubernetes,
  SSH); `feosd` runs the pod's `feos-authn`, which uses them (ADR 0014),
  and the port forwards. No client sees vsock ports.
  Services use them as sandboxes do: a service that deploys over SSH or
  talks to a Kubernetes API gets the access, never the key or the token.

### What a container runs

- A container's process is PID 1 of its container, as with Docker and
  Kubernetes.
- `command` replaces the image's entrypoint and `args` its command, as in
  Kubernetes; each is the image's when empty.
- An **idle** container runs no process of its own. Its PID 1 is FeOS's
  reaper, a small static init mounted read-only like the toolbox, which
  reaps whatever is reparented to it and waits until the guest stops the
  container; no signal from inside the container ends it. Its work comes
  through exec and the tools.
- A container with a process gets the reaper in front of it on request
  (`reaper`), like `docker run --init`. The reaper forwards signals to the
  process, reaps, and exits with the process's exit code.
- Processes started through exec belong to no container's lifecycle: their
  exits change nothing, and they end when their container does.

### A container's role

Every container has one of three roles, and the guest starts them in this
order:

1. **Init** containers run one at a time, in their order, each to its end.
   The next starts only when the one before exited with 0.
2. **Sidecar** containers start next, in their order. Each has started,
   and passed its startup probe if it has one, before the next starts.
3. **Main** containers, the default, start last, together.

Main containers are the pod's work. Sidecars serve them: they keep no pod
alive, and they are stopped after the main containers, in reverse order.

### When a process exits

- The pod's restart policy, `Always`, `OnFailure` or `Never`, says what
  happens when a main container's process exits, as in Kubernetes. It is
  `Always` when unset.
- An init container that fails is run again, unless the policy is `Never`:
  then the pod fails, and nothing after it starts.
- A sidecar is started again whenever it exits, until the pod ends.
- The guest starts a container again, with Kubernetes's backoff: 10 s,
  doubling up to 5 min, reset after 10 min of running. The guest sees an
  exit first, and restarts go on while `feosd` restarts or the control
  plane cannot be reached.
- A container started again keeps its writable layer, as with Docker's
  restart policies: only its process is new. What an agent wrote in its
  sandbox survives a restart.
- A container's status carries its restart count and its last exit.

### When a pod ends

- A pod ends when it has main containers and none of them runs or will
  run again. Its sidecars are stopped, and `feosd` shuts its microVM down,
  which gives its memory back. The pod has succeeded when every main
  container exited with 0, and failed otherwise. Its record keeps each
  container's last exit until the pod is deleted.
- A pod whose main containers are idle, or are always started again, or
  that has none, never ends on its own: it runs until it is stopped,
  suspended or deleted.
- Stopping a pod sends its main containers `SIGTERM`, and `SIGKILL` after
  the pod's grace period, 30 s when unset, as in Kubernetes; then the
  sidecars the same way, in reverse order. Then the microVM shuts down.
- `StartPod` brings an ended pod up again: a new boot, from its init
  containers on.

The products differ in fields, not in kinds:

| | Main container | Restart policy |
|---|---|---|
| Sandbox | idle | `Always` |
| Service | the image's process | `Always` |
| Job | the image's process | `OnFailure` or `Never` |

### Health

- Containers have startup, liveness and readiness probes, as in
  Kubernetes: exec, HTTP GET and TCP. The guest runs them, inside the
  pod's network.
- A failed liveness probe ends the container's process, and the pod's
  restart policy decides what follows. Readiness goes into the container's
  status and the pod's events, for the control plane to route by.

### A pod's services through feosd

A pod's containers, images, tools and watches are reached through `feosd`,
with the APIs they have in the guest (`feos.container.v1`,
`feos.image.v1`, `feos.tool.v1`, `feos.watch.v1`). A call that carries the
metadata `feos-pod-id` goes unchanged to that pod's guest over vsock; a
call without it is for `feosd`'s own services on the host.

### The guest's API is internal

The guest's API is a contract between `feosd` and `feos-guest`, not a
public one (ADR 0007).

### No VMs

FeOS runs microVMs as pods, for CaaS and agent sandboxes, and no VMs of
their own. `feos.vm.v1` is gone from the API: the microVM layer under the
pods is internal to `feosd` (`feos.microvm.v1`, served on no listener),
and what operators had of VMs is a pod's: its console, and its microVM in
`GetPod`. The layer keeps only what pods use: no OCI images with a kernel
of their own, no data disks, no pause, no huge pages.

## Alternatives considered

- **VMs in the API.** Every client would have to know kernels, vsock and
  the guest's protocol, for VMs that have no use but to isolate
  containers.
- **Isolated single containers as the unit.** A pod with one container is
  that; a pod with several also carries sidecars and fits Kubernetes's
  model.
- **Two kinds on the node**, sandboxes and pods, or a kind in the pod's
  spec. `feosd` would know the products, and every capability would be
  decided once per kind. A sandbox with a database as a second container,
  or a service one execs into, fits neither kind.
- **The guest's services re-declared on the host**, as pod-scoped RPCs of
  their own. Every guest API would exist twice; forwarding by metadata
  keeps one.
- **`sleep infinity` for a sandbox's container**, as the sandbox system
  does. It needs `sleep` in the image or the toolbox, and it reaps
  nothing.
- **FeOS's reaper in every container**, since exec is offered on every
  container. Images expect what Docker and Kubernetes do: those built on
  s6-overlay (linuxserver.io's, Home Assistant's) refuse to start unless
  they are PID 1, as they do under `docker run --init`.
- **Containers in the guest's PID namespace**, so that `feos-guest` reaps
  everything. Every container would see the guest's processes and the
  other containers'.
- **Restarts and probes in `feosd` or the control plane.** Every exit and
  every probe would cross vsock and the node's connection, and nothing
  would restart while `feosd` restarts.
- **Sidecars as init containers that restart always**, as Kubernetes
  writes them. One field would mean two things, for a compatibility we do
  not need (ADR 0001). A role says what a container is for.
- **A restart policy per container.** The roles cover what one is wanted
  for, a sidecar that restarts beside a job that does not; Kubernetes, too,
  has one per pod.
- **Keeping an ended pod's microVM**, to exec into what is left and read
  its logs. It would hold its memory until the pod is deleted.

## Consequences

- `feosctl` gets pod commands and a way to name a pod for the guest's
  services, and has no VM commands.
- `ContainerSpec` gains `args`, idle, `reaper`, the role and probes;
  `PodSpec` a restart policy and a grace period; `PodState` a state for a
  pod that succeeded, and failed covers a pod whose containers failed. A
  container's status gains its restarts, last exit and readiness. The
  guest's container API gains the same, additively (ADR 0007).
- `feos-guest` supervises its containers: it starts them in order,
  restarts them, runs their probes, stops the sidecars when the main
  containers are done, and tells `feosd` over its uplink when the pod has
  ended. A suspended pod runs no probes; after a resume they run again at
  once.
- A service that scales to zero is a suspended pod. It is not ready while
  it resumes, so the control plane holds its requests until it is.
- How often a job may fail and how long it may run is the control plane's:
  it sees every restart, and stops the pod.
- Exec into a container whose process does not reap leaves zombies there,
  as with Docker and Kubernetes; `reaper` is the remedy.
- The control plane's CaaS API can take Kubernetes's `command`, `args`,
  `restartPolicy`, init containers, sidecars and probes as they are. A CRI
  adapter that lets kubelet run pods on FeOS nodes would be within reach;
  it is not a goal (ADR 0001).
- The sandbox system's manager splits along this line when it is ported
  (ADR 0003).

## Open questions

- **Interactive exec.** Exec takes no stdin and no terminal; a user's
  shell in a sandbox and debugging a service need both, as such a stream.
- **An ended pod's logs:** gone with its microVM, or their tail kept in
  the pod's record. A job's output matters most when it has ended.
- **Failing fast:** ending a pod when one of its main containers fails,
  without waiting for the others.
- **More credentials for services:** images from private registries pulled
  through the host, which adds the registry's credential, and tokens for
  HTTP APIs added by a proxy on the host.
- **The reaper's binary:** one of its own, or `feos-guest` mounted into
  the container and run again, as for the task service.
- **Background processes** as a capability of the guest: start, list,
  logs and stop. The sandbox system runs them as Python scripts through
  exec, which needs `python3` in the image and keeps their records in the
  container.
